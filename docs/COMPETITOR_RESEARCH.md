# PlugNear competitive research and execution decisions

Research date: **23 October 2026**. Scope: the 20 references supplied by the owner; discovery, host acquisition, access, availability, booking, price, payments, trust, support and business model. Sources below are linked to the exact official pages reviewed or searched. Findings are paraphrased, not copied product specifications.

## What this research establishes

This is a desk review of public websites, official help/policy pages, company announcements and, for Bookmycharge, the UK company register. It is not an authenticated app teardown, hardware test, insurance review, mystery booking, partner API agreement or independent validation of network size. A working marketing site or store listing does not prove transaction volume, charger uptime or that every advertised feature ships. Search snippets were used where fetching failed; those cases are explicitly limited. No competitor accounts, bookings or paid subscriptions were created. No competitor listings, photos, reviews or charger coordinates were imported into PlugNear.

The original list needs qualification: Bolt.Earth's scale claims are self-reported; Statiq is not simply a home-sharing marketplace; EVmatch has announced the end of single-family residential hosting; ElectricPe now emphasizes retail; Bookmycharge Limited is dissolved; Share&Charge is an interoperability reference with old documentation rather than a verified current consumer marketplace. Zyyver's claimed October 2025 launch date was not independently established. These distinctions matter more than collecting another set of homepage animations.

## 1. Bolt.Earth — India

**Evidence:** [official site](https://bolt.earth/), [FAQ](https://bolt.earth/faq), [company story](https://bolt.earth/our-story).

The site describes a distributed P2P network spanning 2W/3W/4W, hardware and management software. Its FAQ distinguishes public paid, public free and private charger visibility, with session and troubleshooting guidance. Network counts vary between the company's own pages and were not independently verified. This is a close India-market comparator, but its hardware/service stack differs from PlugNear's manual pilot.

**Transfer:** explicit host online/offline control, vehicle-specific compatibility and practical charging support. **Do not copy:** national coverage, equipment control, uptime or hardware-backed reliability claims without equivalent operations.

## 2. Statiq — India

**Evidence:** [network](https://www.statiq.in/), [property partnerships](https://www.statiq.in/partner-with-us).

Statiq describes charger discovery/status alongside property partnerships, infrastructure deployment and a franchise-owned/company-operated model. It is a network/operator and property-partner benchmark, not evidence that any home socket becomes an open P2P listing. Its proposition includes ongoing operational management, a substantial distinction from a listing-only marketplace.

**Transfer:** separate individual hosting from commercial site/fleet assessment. **Defer:** franchise investment, turnkey installation and maintenance promises; those require capital, trained partners and contracts.

## 3. EVmatch — USA

**Evidence:** [FAQ](https://evmatch.com/about/faq/), [terms](https://evmatch.com/terms/), [official residential sunset announcement](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/evmatch_evcharging-evadoption-cleantransportation-activity-7438343533620785152-jw9O), [commercial offering](https://evmatch.com/solutions/commercial/).

EVmatch's official announcement says it is sunsetting single-family residential hosting, with residential listings deactivated in early April; vacation rentals are excluded. Its FAQ still explains reservations, advance prices and idle fees. Older P2P descriptions must not be treated as proof of the current scope. Public pages can lag a product transition.

**Transfer:** explicit quote before requesting, booking management and eventual evidence-based overstay handling. **Strategic lesson:** test repeat usage and host retention; a large theoretical number of household chargers is not product-market fit. Do not introduce idle fees without metering, disclosures and enforceable rules.

## 4. Zapmap / Zap-Home / Zap-Work — UK

**Evidence:** [account FAQ](https://www.zap-map.com/faqs/account-faqs/), [current home-charger help article](https://zap-map.freshdesk.com/support/solutions/articles/48001276716-how-do-i-add-my-home-charger-to-zapmap-applies-to-the-uk-only-), [historical launch record](https://www.zemo.org.uk/news-events/news%2Czap-map-launches-peertopeer-home-and-business-charging-network_3770.htm).

Official help search results still expose UK-only home-charger sharing guidance. Zap-Home/Work also have historical launch evidence; that does not establish a current integrated booking/payment product. A later tool fetch of the home-help article failed, so no authenticated flow was verified.

**Transfer:** explain access restrictions and compatibility before travel. **Do not assume:** a discoverable pin means unconditional access, current availability, host acceptance or integrated checkout.

## 5. Co Charger — UK

**Evidence:** [host workflow](https://co-charger.com/become-a-host/how-does-it-work/), [FAQ](https://co-charger.com/faq/), [user terms](https://co-charger.com/co-charger-app-user-terms/).

Co Charger emphasizes dependable repeat relationships with nearby drivers, host acceptance, rate guidance and an attended first session. It distinguishes approximate public map positions from fuller registered-user details. Its FAQ describes a contractual damage-protection policy; this is not permission to imply PlugNear offers insurance or the same cover in India. Published fee and payout language belongs to its own provider arrangements.

**Transfer:** repeat local charging, first-visit handover and realistic host-cost education. **Next architecture decision:** privacy-preserving public discovery and restricted access instructions require backend enforcement, not blurred UI alone.

## 6. Zyyver — UK

**Evidence:** [how it works](https://zyyver.ai/how-it-works/), [FAQ](https://zyyver.ai/faqs/), [host terms](https://zyyver.ai/terms-and-conditions/).

The public proposition combines residential parking and charging and describes smart-plug functionality. Its terms separate platform responsibilities from host permissions, equipment condition and accurate access/fee disclosures. The exact launch date supplied in the brief remains unverified, as do real booking volume and hardware performance.

**Transfer:** treat parking and access as part of charging quality. **Defer:** parking-only inventory and smart-plug switching; both add operational and safety responsibilities beyond PlugNear's current core loop.

## 7. Ivygo — Australia

**Evidence:** [residential hosts](https://ivygo.com.au/hosts/), [business hosts](https://ivygo.com.au/business-hosts/), [FAQ](https://ivygo.com.au/frequently-asked-questions/).

The official site describes advance bookings, host-set rates/availability, manual or automatic approval and platform payments. It segments homes, venues and short-stay accommodation rather than presenting one universal host journey. Payments and verification are its claims; they were not tested. Its market rules do not establish Indian requirements.

**Transfer:** distinguish site types and show a clear host application path. **Keep manual approval:** automatic acceptance should follow reliable calendars, reviewed sites and real operating evidence.

## 8. VLTA — USA

**Evidence:** [product and FAQ](https://vlta.io/), [host app listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/vlta-host-share-your-charger/id6761836587).

The site names NYC Metro, describes separate driver/host experiences, listing onboarding, payouts and schedule overrides. Its FAQ currently says drivers require a Tesla account; Android availability is qualified as forthcoming in parts of the page. It is not evidence of universal vehicle or platform support.

**Transfer:** clear launch geography and explicit compatibility scope. **Do not copy:** account-based telemetry, automatic payout, app-store availability or broad vehicle claims without the actual integrations.

## 9. Voltbee — Netherlands / European context

**Evidence:** [site](https://www.voltbee.app/), [host guide and FAQ](https://www.voltbee.app/share-home-ev-charger), [terms](https://www.voltbee.app/terms).

The site explains home sharing, upfront prices, host control and solar-related economics. Importantly, its host page contains both promotional OCPP connection language and a FAQ saying that integration is not yet available. The stricter qualification should guide interpretation; no deployed remote-control feature was verified.

**Transfer:** explain electricity inputs, losses and costs instead of guaranteed passive earnings. **Do not copy:** savings percentages, earnings projections or unqualified hardware integration claims.

## 10. EV Buddy / Rent-A-Charger — North America

**Evidence:** [EV Buddy site](https://www.evbuddy.net/), [Rent-A-Charger page](https://www.evbuddy.net/rent-a-charger.html), [official company account](https://www.linkedin.com/company/ev-buddy-inc).

The current website foregrounds fleet resilience and mobile/vehicle-to-vehicle energy support, with Rent-A-Charger as another product route. The alternate evchargeshare.net page failed to fetch, but the corresponding official evbuddy.net page was accessible. Neither a presentation nor a pilot announcement proves widespread residential booking supply.

**Transfer:** fleet dwell-time and recovery needs deserve separate operational discovery. **Defer:** roadside response and V2V charging; PlugNear has neither the response organisation nor validated hardware.

## 11. Bookmycharge — UK, historical comparator

**Evidence:** [company register](https://find-and-update.company-information.service.gov.uk/company/10551609), [website attempted](https://bookmycharge.com/).

Companies House lists BOOKMYCHARGE LIMITED as dissolved on **15 April 2025**. The website fetch failed. This verifies the named legal entity's register status, not every possible later owner of the brand or domain. Treat it as historical, not a confirmed operating competitor.

**Transfer:** examine why supply, repeat demand and support economics can fail; do not mistake an early booking concept for proof of a viable current network.

## 12. ChargeZone — India

**Evidence:** [franchise offering](https://www.chargezone.co.in/franchise), [company overview](https://www.chargezone.co.in/about-us).

The franchise page describes infrastructure investment, site/power assessment, deployment and operations. It also warns about misleading subsidy/investment representations. Its commercial model is capital-intensive and materially different from sharing a reviewed existing charger. Marketing ROI figures were not independently validated.

**Transfer:** qualify land, sanctioned load, site suitability and support responsibility. **Do not adopt:** investment returns, subsidy entitlement, finance, highway uptime or turnkey service claims.

## 13. chargeMOD — India

**Evidence:** [site](https://www.chargemod.com/), [residential](https://chargemod.com/charging-solutions/residential), [fleets](https://chargemod.com/charging-solutions/fleets), [channel partners](https://www.chargemod.com/become-a-channel-partner).

The official pages describe residential electrical assessment, workplaces, fleet needs and charging management. Some homepage responses varied between fetches; specific product pages provide stronger evidence for the offering than headline network numbers. Product claims remain untested here.

**Transfer:** site assessment and fleet-specific power/dwell assumptions. **Defer:** CMS, smart energy management and depot operations until real hardware integrations and support capacity exist.

## 14. ElectricPe — India

**Evidence:** [current company story](https://www.electricpe.com/about-us), [contact/support](https://www.electricpe.com/contact-us).

ElectricPe's own story describes a transition from charging aggregation to an EV retail network, with stores and service. It remains relevant to EV adoption and local distribution, but calling it only a public charging network misses its current positioning. Retail/service scale claims were not independently checked.

**Transfer:** local, reachable support and useful driver education. **Do not copy:** retail inventory, warranty, doorstep service or charging partner claims that PlugNear cannot fulfil.

## 15. PlugShare — discovery/community

**Evidence:** [product](https://company.plugshare.com/plugshare.html), [check-in guidance](https://help.plugshare.com/hc/en-us/articles/6368887531283-Check-In-Best-Practices).

PlugShare describes discovery, filters, community photos, check-ins and charging experience comments. Check-in guidance distinguishes what happened at a site and encourages useful access/reliability information. Such reports are user observations, not guaranteed live device telemetry or reserved inventory.

**Transfer:** distinguish host-reported availability, booked status and eventually observed reliability. **Next sprint:** structured session-linked issue reports with moderation and stale-report handling; no fabricated scores or unmoderated automatic delisting.

## 16. Chargemap — European discovery/roaming

**Evidence:** [price page attempted](https://chargemap.com/price), [official pricing help](https://support.chargemap.com/l/en/article/amoapmndfg-how-much-does-charging-cost-with-the-chargemap-pass), [rate differences](https://support.chargemap.com/l/en/article/af1z5uptxi-why-is-the-chargemap-rate-higher-than-the-price-displayed-at-the-charging-station), [charging help](https://support.chargemap.com/l/en/category/nma67tysa5-charging).

Official indexed support explains checking the applicable tariff before charging and that roaming prices can differ from posted station rates. Direct price/help fetches returned access/payment errors; pricing details rely on official search results, not a completed checkout audit. No current pass pricing is quoted here.

**Transfer:** show unit, duration, energy assumption and estimated total before a request. **Defer:** roaming until signed network/data/payment agreements exist. A public map is not permission to aggregate another operator's supply.

## 17. Share&Charge / MotionWerk — interoperability, historical evidence

**Evidence:** [MotionWerk](https://motionwerk.com/), [Open Charging Network FAQ](https://shareandcharge.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/OCN/pages/705724419/Frequently+Asked+Questions), [foundation site attempted](https://shareandcharge.com/).

MotionWerk's site describes blockchain/mobility work. OCN documentation explains OCPI-based backend communication, but the FAQ is dated 2020 and the foundation website was inaccessible. This does not establish a currently operating consumer P2P service or a reason to add blockchain to PlugNear.

**Transfer:** later interoperability design should separate charger control (OCPP) from operator roaming (OCPI). **Do not add:** wallets, tokens or a distributed ledger without a demonstrated operational need.

## 18. Turo — transferable trust operations, not charging

**Evidence:** [trust and safety](https://turo.com/us/en/car-rental/united-states/trust-and-safety), [current terms](https://turo.com/us/en/policies/terms), [review help attempted](https://help.turo.com/en_us/reviewing-your-trip-ByTKNVgEq).

Turo separates eligibility, support, protection products and policy compliance. Public regional/protection pages can disagree as coverage evolves; this review deliberately imports no monetary protection limit. The review help redirect exposed no usable body. Protection must be interpreted through the applicable contract and region.

**Transfer:** explicit verification scope, incident evidence and accountable support. **Do not copy:** insurance language, automatic damage recovery or 24-hour response claims.

## 19. Airbnb — marketplace trust operations

**Evidence:** [identity verification](https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/1237), [location verification](https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/3542), [reviews policy](https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/2673).

Airbnb distinguishes identity and location verification from a guarantee of listing quality or safety, and maintains review rules and dispute processes. These are separate systems, not one generic verified badge. Marketplace identity checks also create sensitive-data handling obligations.

**Transfer:** explain exactly what was reviewed and link reviews to real completed sessions when introduced. **Do not add now:** broad safety guarantees, unmoderated star ratings or unnecessary government-ID uploads without secure evidence handling and retention policy.

## 20. P2P parking — JustPark, YourParkingSpace and ParkPeer examples

**Evidence:** [JustPark owner agreement](https://www.justpark.com/owner-agreement), [reservation explanation](https://support-uk.justpark.com/hc/en-gb/articles/27665904823185-Is-my-space-definitely-reserved), [YourParkingSpace host page](https://www.yourparkingspace.co.uk/list-your-space), [ParkPeer terms](https://parkpeer.app/legal/tos).

This is a category rather than a uniquely identified company named ParkingPeer. The examples disclose access restrictions, owner information and the difference between expected availability and a confirmed reservation. ParkPeer's website/terms are evidence of its stated model, not of booking scale.

**Transfer:** parking size, gates, hours and arrival instructions are essential charger attributes. **Defer:** parking-only reservations, valet/custody and key handling. Never imply the current pilot provides these services or another platform's booking guarantee.

## Product decisions for PlugNear

### Preserve the broad audience; concentrate the launch operation

Keep cars, taxis, bikes, 3W and fleets in the product vision, as requested. Qualify every actual vehicle/site combination. Operate the first pilot in one practical Delhi NCR zone, with real hosts who can honour the same drivers' recurring needs. No vehicle-model compatibility catalogue exists yet; a connector match alone does not establish supported power, adapter safety, parking suitability or OEM approval.

A successful supervised session is stronger proof than a redesigned nationwide map. Do not compete on charger counts before having real supply. Do not buy/build hardware or launch a franchise scheme simply because operators have those products.

### Changes implemented in this research release

- Added a public host preparation guide: permission, installation review, equipment, parking/access, published windows, direct UPI receipt checks, insurance limits and first-session handover.
- Added a local host cost calculator: gross, loss-adjusted electricity input, other assumed costs and contribution. It can show a negative result, rejects invalid assumptions and sends/stores nothing. It does not set a real tariff, forecast demand, promise profits or replace metering/tax advice.
- Replaced the driver's fixed next-hour-only explanation with selectable published window starts and an editable 0.5–12 hour duration. Displays start/end in IST and a paise-rounded estimate; disables requesting when the chosen duration/energy/window is invalid. Server still enforces the reservation and price.
- Added a warning when energy requested exceeds advertised power multiplied by duration; this is an upper-bound planning warning, not a delivery guarantee or vehicle compatibility check.
- Clarified straight-line distance and the distinction between host/booking status and hardware telemetry; added practical pre-arrival/payment guidance.
- Linked the host guide/calculator from the homepage and added its canonical URL to the sitemap.

These changes are deployed; see the validation/deployment record below. They do not establish operational launch readiness.

### Next, with backend and operational design

## Pilot measurement plan

Record consented operational observations, not invented launch numbers: successful signup/confirmation, search → meaningful charger selection → request → host decision → receipt confirmation → completed physical session; failures at each step; detour/dwell time; host availability accuracy; cancellation/no-show counts; support time and repeat use. Keep identities, exact routes and payment references out of general analytics. Choose go/no-go thresholds from a supervised pilot baseline before spending on driver acquisition. Host recruitment can continue with accurate early-access wording.

## Practical launch recommendation

Use the new guide to recruit and prepare a small real host cohort. Complete email delivery and owner/admin setup, then perform the physical acceptance test. Keep broader ads and automated-service promises on hold. Research suggests where to improve, but it cannot prove local demand, legality, insurance coverage, interoperability rights or a bank transfer.
