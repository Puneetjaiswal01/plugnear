# PlugNear strategy: private EVs, taxis, bikes and commercial fleets

## Product decision

PlugNear’s platform scope covers **private electric cars, taxis, bikes/scooters, three-wheelers, delivery vans, buses and other commercial EVs**, including mixed fleets. Potential hosts include homes, local businesses, hotels, workplaces, parking operators and depots. No vehicle group is the exclusive public focus. Pilots should validate vehicle-specific compatibility and local operating needs before services are claimed available. Host-management SaaS remains a later product extension.

The user’s competitive analysis is strategic input, not independently verified market research. Operator counts, competitor coverage, fundraises, API fees and potential fleet customers must be checked before use in public claims or an investment deck. No named logistics company is a PlugNear partner unless a real agreement exists.

## Differentiation to validate

- Useful supply around commutes, taxi shifts, rider routes and fleet depots.
- Vehicle/OEM-charger/supply compatibility across AC/DC and portable-charger use cases, with opening hours and vehicle access.
- Safe parking and reviewed installations at local businesses.
- Repeat fleet access, metered sessions, permissions and cost visibility after the rider/host workflow works.

Do not pursue hardware-heavy highway deployments or promise universal CPO roaming. Use official partner APIs only when commercial rights and technical support are established. Do not scrape availability or imply integration based on a public map listing.

## Business-model hypotheses

Test a transparent fee on verified sessions, then fleet operational software/service fees if repeated fleet use supports that model. Host-management SaaS can reuse proven scheduling, metering and reconciliation infrastructure later. Pricing, take rates, earnings, monthly credit and settlements are hypotheses until merchant, metering, tax and local electricity arrangements are reviewed. No revenue guarantees belong in host acquisition copy.

## Pilot sequence

1. Choose **one operating zone in one city** with an actual fleet/rider cohort; no city or partner is assumed committed.
2. Map rider stops, shifts, dwell time, vehicle/OEM charger models and measured energy needs.
3. Qualify a small number of sites for route fit, hosting permission, parking, electrical inspection and sanctioned load, equipment protection, connectivity and operating hours.
4. Validate actual sessions and metering before enabling payments. Prove reservations cannot overlap, fleet users cannot access another organisation’s records and every payment can be reconciled.
5. Measure repeat riders, useful host density, availability during required windows, successful charging, detour/dwell time, support load, host retention and contribution per settled session. Agree go/no-go criteria from real pilot baselines, not fabricated market metrics.
6. Add fleet billing or priority policies only after session and settlement evidence exists. Credit billing is not just a combined UI invoice; it needs limits, approval and accounting workflows.

## Delivered in this stage

- Public website covers private cars, taxis, bikes/scooters, three-wheelers, commercial EVs and mixed fleets.
- Rider/host messaging explains the compatibility and installation prerequisites.
- Local fleet pilot planner provides editable bike, three-wheeler, car, taxi, van and bus examples; exports name the vehicle group. For mixed fleets, users prepare a brief per group. Inputs stay local.
- Existing app host screen distinguishes current connector-based prototype listings from future socket-based micro-host onboarding.

The existing backend remains a generic charger prototype. No claim is made that 2W/3W compatibility filtering, micro-host review, fleet tracking, organisation billing, smart sockets, live queues or CPO integrations are already operational.
